What is Correspondence Chess?

Correspondence chess is chess played over distance. Historically, moves were exchanged through postcards and letters, often resulting in games lasting months or years. Today correspondence chess is primarily played on dedicated internet servers while preserving the tradition of deep analysis and careful preparation.

Unlike over-the-board chess, players may spend several days on a single move. This creates a unique blend of practical play, research and chess history.

Norway has a proud correspondence chess tradition through the Norwegian Correspondence Chess Federation founded in 1945.

About Arild Haugen

My interest in chess began in Stavanger during the 1950s when my father, the Norwegian magician Ramiras, introduced me to the game.

Over the decades I have participated in national and international correspondence chess tournaments, represented Norway in ICCF events and contributed to preserving the history of Norwegian correspondence chess.

These pages contain selected games, articles, photographs and historical material collected during a lifetime in chess.

Chess Without Borders

Together with Reidar Gramstad and Thor Geir Harestad, I co-authored the book Chess Without Borders, documenting 75 years of Norwegian correspondence chess.

The book contains historical articles, player portraits and 123 memorable correspondence games.

Published for the 75th anniversary of the Norwegian Correspondence Chess Federation, it documents players, tournaments and important milestones in Norwegian correspondence chess history.
