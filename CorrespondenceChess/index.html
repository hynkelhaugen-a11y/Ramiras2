What is Correspondence Chess?

Correspondence chess is chess played over distance. Historically, moves were exchanged by post and games often lasted for months or even years. Today most correspondence chess is played on dedicated internet servers while preserving the tradition of deep analysis and careful strategic planning.

Unlike ordinary tournament chess, correspondence players may spend days analysing a single move. This creates an environment where opening preparation, strategic understanding and endgame technique can be explored in extraordinary depth.

"From stamps and postcards to modern internet servers, the fascination remains the same: finding the best move."

About Arild Haugen

My interest in chess began in the 1950s when my father, the Norwegian magician Ramiras, introduced me to the game. What started as a childhood hobby evolved into a lifelong interest in chess, analysis and correspondence play.

Over several decades I have participated in Norwegian and international correspondence chess events, represented Norway in ICCF competitions and contributed to documenting the history of Norwegian correspondence chess.

Together with Reidar Gramstad and Thor Geir Harestad, I co-authored the book Chess Without Borders, which chronicles 75 years of the Norwegian Correspondence Chess Federation.
