Long Summary

Sverre Halvorsen was one of the most influential figures in Norwegian chess. He distinguished himself as a player, organizer, editor, promoter and mentor. His work helped shape correspondence chess in Norway and strengthened Stavanger's position as a major chess center.

He learned chess as a teenager, became one of Stavanger's strongest players, and contributed to major team successes. During and after World War II he became deeply involved in correspondence chess and helped build the Norwegian Correspondence Chess Federation.

For more than three decades he edited and produced the magazine Postsjakk, making it the central publication for Norwegian correspondence players. He also represented Norway internationally and earned notable competitive results.

Halvorsen worked tirelessly to bring leading chess personalities to Stavanger, including Max Euwe and future world champion Mikhail Tal. His efforts enriched local chess culture and inspired generations of players.

Outside chess he was interested in languages, philosophy, culture and international friendship. Friends and colleagues remembered him as generous, knowledgeable and dedicated.

Honours and Recognition

His contributions were recognized through honorary memberships and widespread admiration throughout the Norwegian chess community. His legacy continues through the institutions, publications and chess culture he helped create.
