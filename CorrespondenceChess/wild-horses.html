The Story Behind the Game

Among the many correspondence chess games played over several decades, Wild Horses remains one of the most memorable. The game involved long-term planning, deep strategic ideas and the type of analytical struggle that makes correspondence chess unique.

The encounter became one of the best known games of my career and has remained a personal favourite over the years.

Wild Horses later attracted the attention of correspondence chess historian Tim Harding and became associated with some of the finest traditions of analytical correspondence chess.

The McNab Game

Wild Horses is closely associated with my game against Colin McNab, one of Scotland's strongest chess players and a respected author and analyst.

The game developed into a rich strategic battle and remains one of the most interesting encounters from my international correspondence chess career.

Correspondence Chess and Deep Analysis

Correspondence chess allows players to spend days analysing critical positions. This frequently produces games of great strategic depth and originality.

Unlike ordinary tournament chess, the emphasis is not only on finding strong moves but also on understanding long-term plans, endgame transitions and hidden positional resources.

Wild Horses remains one of the games that best illustrates these qualities.
